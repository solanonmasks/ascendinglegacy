Critical illness and disability insurance both help if illness or injury changes your life. They work very differently, and if your budget only stretches to one, it helps to know which gap is bigger for you.

## Disability insurance: a monthly paycheque

Disability insurance pays a **monthly benefit** if you cannot work because of illness or injury. It is designed to replace part of your income, for as long as the policy says, while you recover.

- Covers illness *and* injury, including things like back problems and mental health conditions, depending on the policy.
- Benefits usually start after a waiting period, often 30, 60 or 90 days.
- The definition of "disabled" matters: unable to do *your* job, or unable to do *any* job.

## Critical illness insurance: a lump sum

Critical illness insurance pays a **one-time, tax-free lump sum** if you are diagnosed with a covered condition, such as cancer, heart attack or stroke, and survive a short waiting period.

- You can spend it on anything: treatment, the mortgage, a partner taking time off work.
- It pays even if you keep working.
- It only covers the conditions listed in the policy.

## Which first?

A simple way to think about it:

- **If your income pays the bills and you have little savings,** disability insurance often comes first. Most long absences from work are caused by things a critical illness policy does not cover.
- **If you already have good disability coverage through work,** critical illness can fill a different gap: the extra costs and lost family income around a serious diagnosis.
- **If you are self-employed,** you probably have no group coverage at all, so the conversation usually starts with disability.

## Check what you already have

Many employer plans include long-term disability. Look for:

- what percentage of your income it pays
- whether the benefit is taxable
- what happens if you leave the job
